Conference paper and poster

A moderated-mediation analysis of agripreneurial intentions: Assessing agricultural education quality and career perceptions in the Ashanti Region, Ghana

Presented at MEETCON-ANATOLIA, 3rd International Congress of Social and Human Sciences, Gaziantep, Türkiye, 21–25 May 2026

Abstract

Agriculture remains central to Ghana's economy, yet many agricultural science students do not go on to agribusiness careers. This study tested whether career perceptions mediate the link between the quality of agricultural education and students' agripreneurial intentions, and whether that link differs between student groups. Survey data from 274 senior high school agriculture students in eight schools in the Ashanti Region were analysed with PROCESS Models 4 and 7 and 5,000 bootstrap samples.

Education quality had a total effect of β = 0.681 on agripreneurial intentions and explained 51.1% of their variance. It was strongly related to career perceptions (0.622), which were in turn strongly related to intentions (0.751); the indirect effect (β = 0.467) carried about 68.5% of the total. The indirect effect was larger for rural students (0.511) than urban students (0.421), while gender and class level showed no significant difference. The results suggest that agricultural education raises agripreneurial intention mainly by changing how students see farming as a career, and that rural schools may gain most from better facilities and career exposure.
